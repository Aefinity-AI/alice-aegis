## Decisive ablation

### design
```
SAME-SIZE TERNARY/FP ABLATION at 14,171,392 parameters per arm.

WHY IT IS NEEDED: the M7/M7a twin is a FIVE-variable comparison, not the one-variable comparison model.py:166-181 and configs/m7_ternary.json claim. The arms differ in weight precision, parameter count (14,171,392 vs 6,529,920 = 2.17x), peak LR (2e-3 vs 1e-3), LR floor (0 vs 0.1*peak) and weight-decay tail (0 vs 0.1 held). The pre-registered control (git 20516af) isolated the last two and found they alone reproduce 53.8% of the published gap IN THE FP TWIN. The 2.17x parameter difference remains completely untouched, and BitNet b1.58 Reloaded (arXiv:2407.09527), the only study covering 6M/12M/24M/48M LMs, states 1.58-bit effective capacity is roughly HALF of 16-bit - so 2.17x params is the literature's parity FLOOR and the M7 pairing was predicted to be approximately a tie. The residual d_resid = +0.030187 nats is therefore not distinguishable from what the parameter difference alone predicts. The honest current position is that M7 says NOTHING about quantization in either direction.

GEOMETRY: hidden 384, inter 1024, layers 7, heads 6, kv_heads 2, vocab 8192, ctx 512 - the published geometry, unchanged, in BOTH arms. This works because BitLinear adds ZERO parameters: gamma is recomputed from the latent weight at model.py:109-112 and is never an nn.Parameter, so at identical geometry the two arms have byte-identical param counts and identical state_dict keys (verified: sha256 of sorted key:shape identical, nkeys=79 both, delta=0 at four separate geometries). No compensation of any kind - flip one JSON key. This geometry is also chosen because the fp32 tied embedding is UNQUANTIZED in both arms and is pure dilution of the effect: at 14,171,392 params the non-embedding fraction is 77.8%, the highest of any affordable rung, versus 66.7% at 9,446,272 and 51.4% at 4,313,664.

SHARED INIT (the confound nobody has fixed): with the same --seed the two arms do NOT currently get the same initialization. nn.Linear.__init__ calls reset_parameters() (kaiming_uniform_) BEFORE make_linear overwrites the weight with normal_(0,0.02) at model.py:179, consuming torch RNG that BitLinear (torch.empty at model.py:144) does not. The embedding matches (built first) but every projection diverges - verified: 'embed_tokens identical across arms: True', 'layer0.q_proj.weight identical: False'. FIX, requiring NO edit to train.py (which roundtrip_gate.py imports): write a step-0 checkpoint from the bitlinear arm and start BOTH arms with --resume from a private copy of it. Because state_dicts are key- and shape-identical, load_ckpt transplants the init exactly, and --resume also restores the torch RNG state so both arms enter step 0 identically. The fp arm still trains as fp because cfg comes from argparse, not from the checkpoint. Verified end to end: 'fp state_dict identical AFTER transplant: True, resumed step=0'; 'fp q_proj output == plain F.linear (no fake-quant): True' at rtol=0, while ternary q_proj output differs.

SCHEDULE - both arms get --sched two_stage. This is the whole point: train.py:167-174 branches the recipe on `sched` alone with NO linear-mode branching anywhere, so two_stage applies the LR->0 anneal AND the wd->0 drop at the 50% knee IDENTICALLY to an fp arm. The lever today's control proved is worth 53.8% of the published gap is thereby applied symmetrically instead of asymmetrically, and it requires ZERO code change. DELIBERATE DESIGN DECISION: do NOT bundle a WSD schedule into this run even though a WSD trunk would be independently valuable. Adding an untested schedule to the one experiment that must not fail is exactly the failure mode this program is trying to stop. Instead, step-tagged checkpoints (L02) give branch points at matched schedule positions, including one at the knee (step 34,500, lr 1e-4) that serves as a perfectly good cooldown branch point.

PEAK LR - the one hyperparameter that cannot be made simultaneously fair. Published values were 2e-3 (ternary) and 1e-3 (fp), and there is NO on-box LR-sensitivity data for either arm at any scale. Pre-register 1e-3 for BOTH: it is the fp arm's own published choice, it handicaps ternary, and it makes a 'ternary is fine' verdict conservative. Supporting evidence that 1e-3 is not unreasonable for ternary at this scale: BitNet's 'use ~2x the fp LR' tip comes from 700M-3.9B, while BitNet b1.58 Reloaded, testing 6M-48M, found the opposite - small quantized networks were UNSTABLE at high LR with optimal rates of 0.001-0.0001. Add a CONDITIONAL third arm (ternary at 2e-3, +80.8 h) run ONLY if ternary loses by more than the equivalence bound.

BUDGET - 69,000 steps x 8 x 512 = 282,624,000 tokens = 19.94 tokens/param, 0.53 epoch of train_8k.bin's 536,203,430 tokens, so no repetition. Chinchilla-optimality is NOT required and should not be paid for: Chinchilla answers how to split a fixed budget between N and D, and here N is pinned by construction. ~20 tok/param is the right floor; below ~10 the contrast starts measuring optimization speed rather than converged quality. The 500M-token option (matching the published endpoints) costs 263 h and buys almost nothing, because the endpoints it would match are single-512-token-window numbers whose instrument jitter is roughly half the effect they reported.

BIAS DIRECTION, pre-registered: a truncated budget biases AGAINST ternary, because the standard QAT finding is that ternary lags early and closes late, and Reloaded's 2x-width result is a FLOOR statement meaning small-scale results understate ternary. So 'ternary is fine at 283M tokens' transfers upward; 'ternary is worse at 283M tokens' does NOT settle whether 500M closes it.
```

### what_it_settles
```
1. Whether ternary QAT costs quality at MATCHED PARAMETERS, at the exact geometry that ships on the aegis-core engine, under a schedule applied IDENTICALLY to both arms. This is the question the M7 headline was thought to have answered and did not.

2. It settles it at the shipped artifact's own scale (14,171,392 params, 77.8% of parameters in quantized projections) rather than at a cheap proxy, so it speaks directly to the DARPA story and to the model that actually boots on the engine.

3. It removes ALL FIVE known twin confounds simultaneously: weight precision is the only difference; parameter count is identical by construction (BitLinear adds zero parameters); peak LR is identical at 1e-3; LR floor is identical (both anneal to 0 under two_stage); the weight-decay tail is identical (both drop to 0 at the 50% knee); and initialization is identical by TRANSPLANT rather than merely same-seeded, which fixes a divergence that same-seeding does not.

4. It also largely neutralizes the SEVENTH, previously unlisted confound - the gradient-clip asymmetry, measured at 77.6% clip rate on the fp twin versus 31.2% on the ternary - because at identical geometry the arms' gradient norms are far more comparable, and the post-clip effective LR will now be logged rather than inferred.

5. It produces the first ternary checkpoint set with step-tagged mid-run snapshots, converting all future ternary schedule research from 80-hour runs into ~4.7-hour branches.

6. It produces a gap-versus-tokens CURVE for free from matched-position snapshots, since both arms run the identical schedule.
```

### what_it_does_not_settle
