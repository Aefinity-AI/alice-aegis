## Clean

**`02_related_work.md`** — clean. All 6 spot-checked arXiv citations (out of
5+ required) resolve to real papers whose titles, authors, and claimed
figures (e.g. Wang et al.'s 2.37–6.17×/1.37–5.07× CPU speedups) match what's
attributed to them. No CIS-1 ledger numbers are cited in this section to
cross-check. One low-materiality framing point, not logged as a table row to
avoid padding: "None of this line of work specifies a bit-exact numerical
contract..." is a strong, unqualified claim about the *absence* of a property
across an entire literature — true as far as I can check, but it's an
assertion about what other papers *don't* do, which is harder to falsify than
what they *do* claim, and is exactly the shape of statement a hostile
reviewer will try to find one counterexample against.

## Three most important

1. **`07_why_it_matters.md`'s "2B model on a decade-old laptop with no
   operating system"** — the single most damaging finding. No ledger row
   supports it; it directly contradicts spec §9's explicit "2B full-integer
   quality is not yet measured," and BitNet-2B has never run on the no-OS
   unikernel at all (only M7 has, via A32–A34).
2. **`01_introduction.md`'s "+0.74%... all-integer forward pass"** mislabels
   the BitNet-2B **hybrid** result as full-integer, contradicting
   `CIS-1_SPEC_v1.0.md` §9 verbatim — and the error traces back to
   `table3_provenance.md`'s own row 6 label, so it's a propagated error, not
   an isolated slip.
3. **`07_why_it_matters.md`'s blanket "faster... on the hardware where it was
   measured"** — directly contradicted by A26's Dell scalar result (integer
   25% *slower* than float on Broadwell-U); the paper's own introduction
   states the nuanced (correct) version two sections earlier, so §7
   contradicts §1.
